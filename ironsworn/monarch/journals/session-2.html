Session 2

Fey hadn't been on Meridian long enough to accumulate much more than with what she arrived: a duffel bag of spare clothes, a toothbrush, and a sword. Her spacer's jumpsuit was bulky enough to take the rest of the remaining space in her bag, but she'd taken to wearing it ever since leaving the Order to hide the blackiron gall tattoos that would have screamed "exiled Blade." It was hard enough finding work when everyone thought the Marked could just read every thought on a whim.

Emotions and vague intents, sure. Fey cinched her sash around her waist and pulled an old, well-worn jacket over her shoulders. It was made of some kind of synth-leather with enough creases and rubbed out stains that it must have had some kind of story. Fey didn't know. She just found it in a thrift shop. It was the right size and cropped short enough to easily access her sword. It was an extra bonus that every time she'd sensed an emotion that could be interpreted as seeking easy prey quickly flashed to thoughts of steel and disappointment.

Anything more detailed like actual thoughts and memories required taking a hit of rust, and having the mental capacity to sift through that chaotic mess took a second hit or more. Fey had to admit that she enjoyed the crystal clear vision and understanding of how to literally cut fate that rust gave her... but it also made her all too aware of how easy it would be to become a blubbering mess with her expanded brain leaking out of her ears.

With a final zip of her bag, the steel box Fey had been renting was once again empty save for the (surprisingly comfortable) bed and (unsurprisingly threadbare) blankets. These particular dormitories seemed to have been a converted cargo hauler. Fifth wave, if Fey had to guess. Meridian was the only thing of note orbiting the system's white dwarf which had died ages ago. Even the asteroids had been blown away, and so without any materials, Meridian was built from strata of ships, welded together over time to make a bustling trade port. If it weren't for the intersecting leylines between this sector and the other and the next, the Meridian system would have been a footnote in an post-Exodus science book. Instead, it was the first and last stop in and out of the entire sector.

The Elara Five was docked not too far from Fey's dorm, straightforward enough save for the one passage where the grav-plating was fully 90° from each other. Soon enough, Fey found herself in one of the many commercial spires: a long wide hallway with airlocks on either side with plenty of hands moving goods in and out. It was the usual mess of activity, but Aparna was easy enough to spot a few airlocks down smoking a cigarette.

Fey had clocked Aparna's bright longcoat and scarred eye when they were in Argus' office, but the woman had left promptly. This was the first time Fey could properly assess who she'd been paired up with. Aparna's hair was greying but not quite grey, and the wrinkles around her eyes spoke of weary experience. She was shorter than Fey, but as she saw the young woman approach, Aparna straightened up, carrying herself with tall with the easy confidence of a captain that had been around more than once.

"Ms. Zariri!" Aparna put her cigarette into a box then slipped it into an inner pocket of her coat. "Right on time."

"Fey is fine, if you don't mind."

"Great. You can call me Aparna, too. Let's get us onboard and on our way."

Aparna waved her PDA over the airlock's auth panel, and the first door hissed open. The docking tube was transparent along the sides, and as the two women floated through it, Fey saw the Elara Five for the first time.

She was shaped like a wide wedge, bulging in the back with its twin torch drives flanking the massive eidolon array. If it weren't for the bright red and turquoise geometric patterns painted along the hull, the Elara Five could pass for any of the other courier ships that flitted through the lanes. These ships were light, fast, and roomy enough for a small crew to stay sane while jumping between sectors. Information needed to get around the Forge, and so they were everywhere.

The docking tube connected to the Elara Five's ventral port, and with another wave of Aparna's PDA, another hatch hissed open. With practiced ease, Aparna took hold of a rung at the lip of the hatch and floated in, gracefully pivoting into a squat on the other side. She held a hand out.

"Let me take your bag, the grav-plate's on." Fey handed Aparna her bag and with significantly less grace, scrambled onto the ship. "Welcome aboard. Let me show you to your quarters."

The belly of the Elara five was a modest cargo hold with a modest load. Aparna slung Fey's bag over her shoulders and took a ladder up. Fey followed her into a narrow corridor. To one end, Fey could glimpse consoles and monitors for what must be the cockpit, and to the other, Fey could hear the dull hum of the ship's reactor. Aparna started going toward the cockpit, but stopped at the first door to starboard. She hit a button and the door slid open.

The room was... nice. It had a bed, a desk, a warm lamp to light the room, and even a rug. Aparna set Fey's bag next to a wardrobe.

"Make yourself at home," Aparna said. "The galley's on the port side of the engine and the head's right before it. The Spider paid your supply bonus upfront so don't worry about food."

"Thank you." Fey was genuinely pleased. At the same time, she was apprehensive. Before the captain could leave, Fey pulled a small pouch from the folds of her sash. "I figure you already clocked me as Marked. Here's my rust. I'll need it back once we're on mission, though."

Aparna tilted her head. "Now why would I want to hold onto your drugs?"

"Captains usually don't like a mind reader onboard unless they're doing the nav charts."

"Honey, I'm an open book." Aparna let out a loud laugh. "Only secrets I'm keeping are whatever I'm smuggling, and right now that's nothing. Tap me if you want, but I promise my stories are more fun if you let me embellish them and gloss over the embarassing parts."

Fey furrowed her brow.

"I get it, you're like one of them kittens in a new house. Tap me if you want, but I trust you not to delve too deep." The captain spread her arms. "Really, go on."

Fey furrowed her brow further, but after a moment, did as she was told. She didn't need to take any rust to dip a toe into the waters, and so she took a breath and slowly exhaled, letting that itch in the back of her head and the butterfly in her stomach have their say. For a brief moment, Fey vaguely felt what Aparna felt: a fading buzz of nicotine, aloof confidence fighting a background angst, mild amusement, and above all: curiosity. Fey could only speculate what about, but it was one of the best options she could hope for. At least there wasn't any obvious malice.

"Ok, maybe I shouldn't have been so blasé about that," Aparna said with a laugh. "Y'all's eyes get so spooky when you do that."

"Sorry."

"I can't tell you to stop worrying about it, but stop worrying about it, kid. I can tell every captain you've worked with until now only wanted dumb muscle. We're about to step into a clan arkship to look for someone who, for all we know, kicked over a hornet's nest. The Spider is a smart man. If he thought all I needed was dumb muscle, he wouldn't have paired me up with former Ashen Blade."

"How did you—"

"Like I said, don't worry about it!" Aparna laughed even louder. "If the Spider thought all you needed was transportation, he wouldn't called a former spy. We have plenty of time to chat about it on the way. Get yourself situated and find me on the bridge."

Aparna Solas once went by the callsign "Hellion" and was a field intelligence officer for the Serene League until she lost her eye on mission. "Half her face," Aparna boasted. Like all Founder Clans, the Serene League's arkship had the medtech to grow the eye back, but Aparna said that incident made her realize she was slowing down enough to retire.

"If you think you're slowing down," Fey started, "why take this job?"

"I owe the Spider," Aparna said. She went distant for a moment. "We fed each other a lot of info back in the day. More than once he'd give me extra tips, on the house, solely to keep me breathing."

"That was kind of him."

"Calculated, more like," Aparna said. "But what's the difference between friends?"

There were no windows on the bridge, or anywhere else on the ship for that matter. Prolonged exposure to the drift caused nausea and headaches, as well as a rising sense of dread that no one listed as an official symptom but everyone knew was real. Looking directly at the chaos energies only made the problem worse.

Thankfully, safer routes were found, colloquially called "leylines" because the Marked that plotted them looked like oracles in a trance from all the rust they took to expand their minds. The nausea and headaches and dread would still come, but Fey couldn't feel anything yet. Looking at the monitors, they'd be dropping out of the drift soon, anyway.

"So, kid," Aparna said. "I know I spooked you real good by calling you out as an Ashen Blade, but tap me again if you want, that's really all I figured out about you."

"I won't do that. I trust you there, but how did you know?"

"You changed the colors on your sword, and you hide the tattoos well. Plenty of swordsmen around the Forge, and you'd be surprised at how many are also Marked, but not many who are taught courtly courtesy."

"The way I put down my sword can't only be practiced in the Ashen Star systems."

"Nah. I just took a guess, and you reacted." Aparna winked.

Fey made a bemused laugh. "Got me. You gonna turn me in?"

"Not worth my time. I am curious, though."

"A lot of people don't trust Marked as it is; it goes double for founder clan agents," Fey said. "Even if I'm not one anymore, most don't know that."

"Oh that part, I get. I want to know what you did."

Fey felt her stomach do a flip, and she was pretty sure it wasn't because of drift exposure. She pushed away the screams. "I... wasn't able to do my duty. Too much rust. A lot of people got hurt because of that."

"Ah, sorry I asked."

"It's... fine. It hasn't happened since." Fey took a breath. "Anyway, it looks like we're almost here."

Aparna nodded and turned her full attention to the monitors in front of her. Her hands danced across her console, and soon, Fey felt the weight of the drift suddenly lift from her entire being. It was like a frog boiling in cold water: you didn't notice until it started to hurt, but once you were pulled out, you realized just how much better life could be. Aparna took a satisfied deep breath, then hit a series of buttons, flipping the monitors to view the outside.

Most of the field of view was taken by Penumbra, a lush green world with sparkling oceans, sallow clouds, and an atmosphere that could kill a human with half a breath. Aparna tapped a few keys, zooming in on a speck in orbit that turned out to be Crux, the arkship that had first brought the Suns of the Stellar Commonwealth to the Forge.

Only in front of a planet did Crux seem small. It had shepherded hundreds of thousands during the Exodus, and still today, tens of thousands called it home. Humanity had hoped to find a new planet to call home in the Forge; unfortunately, most planets were dead, and if they weren't, they were like Penumbra: toxic and deadly to simply exist on, even if you ignored the forgespawn. It didn't stop most of the Founder clans from trying to colonize them.

Fey vaguely remembered from her studies that the Suns had been working around Penumbra for at least a century, but the most important thing they had discovered in their futile efforts to terraform was how to bring the local flora back to the void. Crux's hull was covered in green and yellow fields. The local crops flourished when exposed directly to the Forge's chaotic energies, and after several generations of genetic modification, while humanity hadn't been able to live planetside, hunger was at least solved.

"Arkship Crux, this is the Elara Five. Do you read?"

"Elara Five, this is Arkship Crux. We read you."

"Transmitting courier codes and requesting docking clearance. I got a lotta mail for you all."

"Courier codes received. Verifying."

Aparna drummed her fingers.

"Codes verified, Elara Five. Port rho-seven-seven is cleared for docking."

Fey took the hour before docking to prepare. Nothing seemed amiss, but if Argus was nervous, Fey couldn't pretend to hope everything would be fine. She tied her blackiron sword into her sash then regarded her pouch of rust. Rust wasn't illegal if you had the permits, but as an exile, Fey didn't have the permits. Aparna knew this, too, and had banged twice on a specific panel in Fey's wall, revealing a hidden space where she could keep her stash. Fey took out one of the glass tubes before putting the rest into hiding.

When pure blackiron was exposed directly to the void, it would slowly crystalize. Pulverize that down, mix it with several other questionable additives, and you got the iridescent amethyst powder known as rust. It moved wrong for a powder: more like oil if it could coat the air.

Rust was why Fey had made her way to Meridian in the first place. Sources of blackiron weren't easy to come by in the Forge, and when they were found, forgespawn were never far away. One of the settlements in the Morien Deep had figured out a decent enough extraction scheme, and Meridien had a thriving black market. The only problem was that the ship Fey had contracted onto was heading toward Ashen Star space, so Fey had to bid them farewell with as little explanation as possible.

There was enough rust in each vial to kill a Non-Marked ten times over. It should be plenty for the mission. Fey loaded the vial into her combat mask. The mask's internals were subtly designed: on casual inspection, it looked like a spacer's mask, something easily worn on the hip and sealed the bare minimum of the head to keep it from boiling if there was sudden exposure to vacuum and keep you alive long enough to find a real suit. If you bit this mask's air feed in just the right way, though, it could mix a dose of rust right into your air supply.

Externally, the mask was a dome of seemingly opaque titanium that covered her face completely. Bright orange and black veined wings were painted on it. Monarch. A name Fey once went by. She sighed, then clipped the mask opposite her sword and made her way to the bridge.
