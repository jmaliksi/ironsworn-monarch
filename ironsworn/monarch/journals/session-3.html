Session 3

Flying up to an Exodus Exodus Arkships was dizzying in more ways than one. There was the size, but Fey had been vatborn and raised on an arkship herself, and during her years of training, she had flown in and out enough times to get used to the scale. However, arkships were built and launched in an era before gravplate, and so they kept the gravity on the old fashioned way: by spinning. They didn't spin much, but it was noticeable especially when flying into their maw and matching velocity to dock.

The Elara Five's autopilot brought them into a busy commercial dock. Despite the activity, the scale of the arkships made everything feel less cramped. Looking up and around, the walls curved with green fields of ecosystems plucked from the Old Stars and cities with foundations just as old. Orderly shuttle traffic buzzed through the air. Despite the age, everything was well kept—dirty and worn from use, not from neglect, and not for lack of retrofits. The central strut that spanned the entire arkship and where spaceborn ships came and went had long since been refitted for gravplate.

As soon as Aparna and Fey had taken one step onto the terminal, a robed dignitary with two guards greeted them.

"Welcome, Courier Solas." The dignitary bowed, the beads of his sunburst veil clacking together with the movement. "Allow us to escort you and your aide to the communication hub."

Aparna gave a broad smile. "That'd be lovely. Though, we are on a bit of a time crunch so Misha has to run resupply while we get all this distributed. Can one of your boys take the load off her?"

The dignitary paused almost imperceptibly. Fey felt a wisp of confusion and surprise, but they quickly melted away. "Of course, Courier Solas. Please."

Fey gladly set her crate of mail on the floor. Aparna patted her on the shoulder. "Off you go."

Fey gave a shallow bow and pulled up a map of Crux on her PDA. The ship was broken up into wards, each brimming with a thousand or more people. Personal shuttles buzzed overhead, but according to the intel, Alisa was located in ward 331-C which had a direct transit tube from the terminal. While Fey could afford the shuttle fare, she didn't particularly want to pay whatever stupid prices they'd definitely demand.

The train came quick enough, and it was empty enough for Fey to grab a seat where she didn't think anyone could look over her shoulder. She scrolled through her PDA, reviewing her intel.

The Goldpact Guns were a mercenary outfit hired as local guards in ward 331-C. Mercenary work was popular in the Forge. By treaty the Founder Clans didn't have standing armies and were forbidden to make articles of conscription. Fullscale war was something everyone hoped to be rid of after the Sun Plague destroyed the stars and forced the remnants of humanity to the Forge. Unfortunately, ideals lasted no longer than the first encounter with forgespawn. Rules were bent and blind eyes were turned as independent mercenary companies sprung up, selling their services whenever the Founder Clans needed more than a hundred guns pointed somewhere.

Whether by hook or by crook, independent "peacekeepers" were hired for local affairs, and the Goldpact Guns were an unofficial subsidiary to one of the main outfits hired to police Crux. They were new to the scene, only being hired on in the last year, but news reports indicated that they were below professional standards, even for mercenaries. Claims of extortion and destruction of property had been raised, but never seemed to gain any ground. Something must have snapped recently, because several of their lieutenants had been found shot in short order.

Fey worked her jaw in annoyance. These were small fries leaning on innocent people because they'd been tossed scraps. At the same time, she wasn't sure if they deserved to die.

It was hard to kill someone when you could hear all their final thoughts.

Before too long, Fey's train came to a stop at ward 331-C. The ward looked to be an industrial zone, with wide streets meant for trucks and squat, utilitarian buildings. According to the intel, the Goldpact Guns had an unofficial office in one of these warehouses. A yellow flag at the end of one particular service road told Fey she found the right place.

The young woman paused before turning down the road to steady herself. She closed her eyes and took a breath. The hairs on the back of her neck stood, and the itch at the back of her head reverberated only with dull white noise. Then she put on her mask and bit down.

Rust smelled of burnt meat and existential dread. For Non-Marked, shortly after registering the smell, one would get hit with fractal hallucinations of nonsense and a simultaneous high of every emotion imaginable. For the Marked, it was clarity.

Every speck and line and hue screamed into Fey's vision, but to her mind, it was world laid bare. It was what the universe was like beyond simple rods and cones, and as a Marked, it felt right.

Among the deluge was a piqued curiosity under a fight-or-flight response from the one guard who sat outside the warehouse's door. With her rust sharpened mind, Fey also heard a name: Magnus Bridger.

"That's a fun mask," the man said. He swaggered out of his guard booth, making an obvious show of the pistol at his hip. "Here trying to get some treats?"

Fey rested her offhand on her sword, pausing to make sure the man clocked it. "You're Magnus Bridger, right?"

Magnus didn't hide his shock. "What do you want?"

"I know Alisa Shepherd is being held here. You're going to hand her over to me."

"A-and why would I do that?"

Fey let the silence hang a moment. "Are you sure you're being paid enough to ask that question?"

Fey felt an internal war from Magnus' thoughts that finally collapsed into resignation. She almost felt sorry for the guy.

Magnus looked over his shoulder. "If I cooperate, you won't kill me?"

"What made you think I was going to kill you at all?" Fey said flatly.

"That Shepherd bitch is crazy, and if you're running in the same circles as her then lots of things are telling me you're going to kill me. I can get you to her cell, pretend you're a prisoner or something, but then I walk away and you two can do whatever the hell you want. Deal?"

"I think that's reasonable."

"I have to hold your sword, though," Magnus choked. "For the prisoner bit. Please?"

If it was an act, it was a good one because stray thoughts of genuine panic kept flicking off his heart. Fey cocked her head and saw him twitch. Then she pulled her sword from her sash and handed it to Magnus. "Let's go."

"Should I tie your hands—"

"No. Just push me in the back in the right direction."

"Right."

For all his stammering, Magnus was convincing enough once the ruse was going. They passed a pair mercenaries playing cards at the front desk, and he didn't even stutter when mumbling "new prisoner" to which his colleagues shrugged. Fey could feel a bit of his confidence rising as they got deeper into the building.

Behind the reception room was a corridor of linoleum floors and recycled aluminum walls. They passed a door with a pair of clerks lost in whatever logistics was scrolling down their monitors, another door to an empty break room, and stopped to enter a third door.

There was only one cell in this room, and behind the iron bars sat a pale woman glaring at the door through shaggy blonde hair. She was dressed in a gray pantsuit as if she had just left an office job, though the tailoring was lost under the sweat stains.

"Who the fuck are you?"

"Alisa Shepherd?" Fey asked.

"No, that's me. I asked who you were."

"The Spider sent me."

Alisa visibly relaxed, then suddenly she lurched forward, grabbing the bars. "Then what are you waiting for?"

Something was off... Fey let her rust sharpened mind fall back into the universe's lap, opening herself to Alisa's mind.

The serenity and focus in Alisa Shepherd's mind rammed Fey like a misaligned docking procedure. Fey expected anger at her captors or weariness from being detained for so long, but instead it was like a single held note. It might diving deeper into the woman's memories easier.

A dead dropped parcel. Names and faces. Chits. No note. Coy remarks, dark places, flashes of light and blood. No eyes, sure of it. Grabbed anyway.

Alisa was the killer, but the Goldpact Guns couldn't prove it which was why she wasn't dead herself.

Who tipped them off? Who ordered the hits?

With more rust, Fey could delve further, sift through the evidence, piece the wider web together and fill the hole the Spider threw them into... Memories of feeling a hundred souls screaming scratched the doors of her conscious. No. Fey suppressed a shiver.

"Magnus," Fey said. "Unlock the cell and walk away. Deal's a deal."

A dozen futures flashed through Fey's mind in a single fractalized moment, and the will to make exactly one coursed through her body, carried almost more by the blackiron gall tattoos than her own nerve endings.

Fey took two quick steps toward Magnus, and to the stammering man's credit, he was quick to draw his gun and shoot at a space Fey would have occupied if she hadn't made a quarter turn to the left. One more step in, and Fey had crushed Magnus' wrist before he could squeeze the trigger a second time and spun him into a headlock. A few gasps later, Magnus was crumpled on the floor.

"You didn't kill him," Alisa said, still coming out of her cell.

"Did he deserve to die?" Fey relieved Magnus' body of her sword. She held the man's gun out to Alisa.

Alisa took the weapon and checked it with proficiency. "Nah. But they're mercs pretending to be cops. They cause more problems and solve none. Who gives a shit?"

A shout from the hallway saved Fey from having to respond.

Alisa pressed herself against the door, peeking out. "Two incoming."

Fey saw Alisa shift her weight, about to lean out and shoot. There wasn't honor in killing idiots. It wasn't worth the pain.

Fey dashed out the door and into the hallway, intentionally knocking Alisa back into the room. Down the hall, the two mercs who had been playing cards were running toward them, guns drawn. They started shooting. Fey wouldn't give them enough time.

Only once the shuttle was in the air did Fey let out a breath. She could feel the rust fading, and the universe quieting down to simple linear time. The snatches of stray thought and emotion ebbed to vague itch in the back of her mind. Fey could feel her hands shaking as she undid her mask.

Alisa took a moment to study Fey's face. Her gaze lingered on the Marked's eyes. "Interesting. You know, I'd love to ask more but these shuttles tend to..." Alisa tapped her ears.

The ride passed in silence. Fey took a moment to message Aparna, simply texting "on our way", but otherwise the two looked out different windows twiddling their thumbs. They got dropped off one terminal down from where the Elara Five was actually berthed.

Aparna was waiting for them with a cigarette in her mouth. "Nice job grabbing the supplies. Need a lift?"

"Can't stay here, apparently."

"Aparna Solas, and that pretty lady is the Elara Five." The woman scrolled her PDA and held it up. "I'd give you the whole spiel and tour but judging by the chatter we should get going before someone has the bright idea to lock the terminal."

The trio wasted no time boarding and getting to the bridge. Apparently Aparna had already warmed the engines, and so only after a terse wait for clearance, the Elara Five was unclamped and headed back out to the void, and none too soon.

The withdrawals hit Fey with a second wave of shakes and shivers. The woman clenched her teeth and let herself slump onto a flight couch.

"You're looking rough, kid." Aparna reached under her console to a mini fridge and pulled out a drink pouch. She held it out at Fey, her other hand and her eyes still locked on the departure procedure.

Fey groaned as she leaned forward, but Alisa took the pouch and thankfully handed it over. "You should see what she did to the other guys."

"Just knocked them out," Fey fumbled at the screw cap.

"Hard mode, if you ask me." Alisa leaned on the far wall. "So, I got the captain's name. What's yours?"

"Fey."

"And the Spider thought I was important enough to send his new Marked?"

"Guess so."

Alisa glanced at Aparna as her hands flew over the console, then locked the steering yoke into place. Aparna turned her chair toward the two women. "We're clear of Crux. Leyline's just gonna be an hour out and we're back at Meridian."

"Great." Alisa locked her eyes back on Fey. "Looks like it takes a lot out of you. What's that like?"

Fey rolled her neck. "Like being blind again."

"That's good." Alisa pulled out Magnus' gun and shot Aparna.
