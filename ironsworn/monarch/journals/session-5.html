Fey's head pounded in protest from the exertion. Her rust was floating down the hallway, she could just—

Aparna had been shot. Alisa's stump was turning into an orb of blood. Fey squeezed her eyes in an attempt to focus. She took a breath. Aparna first.

Fey somersaulted her feet into the ceiling and pushed herself toward Aparna. The spy was splayed out in the air, groaning and coughing in equal order. "Should probably stop the bleeding if you want to question her, kid."

"She can go to hell," Fey said, catching Aparna and gingerly floating them both to the nearest console. Fey hooked a foot under the chair and began scrounging underneath for something to staunch the bleeding.

"Left cabinet," Aparna said, breathing heavy. "Look. I'm not gonna make it."

"Save your energy." Fey threw a several boxes and books out of the cabinet before finding a white plastic case labeled "first aid." She tore out a roll of gauze and immediately began pressing the whole thing into Aparna's stomach. To the spy's credit, she only grimaced.

"No, you save your energy and put it in the right place. My insides are torn up. Grav's off so even if you did know how to stitch up a spleen, the blood would never drain."

"But—"

"I'm selfish and I'm not done talking. If I'm dying I want whoever killed me to pay."

"You're not dying. We can turn this ship around. Arkships can fix anything. You got half your face blown off, remember?"

Aparna started to laugh then made a face of immediate regret. "Shut up, kid. You know as well as I do that there are more enemies than friends waiting to kill or capture us and to get Alisa back on her feet."

Fey opened her mouth to protest but stopped.

"Consider it my dying wish for you to interrogate that bitch and make her boss regret whatever scheme they've cooked up. Got it?"

Her dying wish. How could Fey ignore that? The young woman felt her vision blur with undrained tears. She wiped them into her sleeve as best she could then pulled out her sword.

"Aw, you don't have to do that."

"Yes I do." Fey slid an inch of her blackiron blade out of its sheath and cut her thumb. She pressed a bloody print into the blade. "I swear to find your killer, Aparna Solas. By iron and blood and void."

Aparna closed her eyes and grinned. "By iron and blood and void. It does kinda fill you with a certain je ne sais quoi when you put it that way."

Fey squeezed Aparna's hand. Aparna squeezed it back. A moment passed.

"Alright," Aparna said, letting go of Fey. She winced as she reached into her pocket for her PDA. "Much as I'd like to drift off in the arms of my heir apparent, Alisa's just about bled out so you got work to do. And I've gotta draft one last letter for Argus. Fulfill your vow, Monarch."

Fey was taken aback. Monarch. But there was no time to think about that. The young woman snatched the first aid kit and turned back toward the floating unconscious body of Alisa Shepherd.

In microgravity, the blood from Alisa's stump was crawling up her arm. The woman's grey blouse was nearly soaked fully through. Fey pulled a strip of rubber from the kit and tied it around Alisa's forearm, cinching it as tight as she could. She then started pushing a new roll of gauze into the wound itself.

The assassin's breath was ragged and her skin was paler than pale. What if she already lost too much blood? Fey tore open a new pack of gauze to pile onto the already soaked bandaging.

"Aparna, you got a med-bay I can at least strap both of you into? Something more than just this kit? Aparna?"

Fey turned back toward the spy to confirm what she already knew: she was dead. Aparna's corpse floated without any grace, shirt soaked through with blood and skin pale from lack of the same. Her chest was still and her mouth was agape. Her PDA floated just beneath her slackened fingertips.

This was all too much. Fey knew this job was dangerous, but she'd taken dangerous jobs in the past. She'd walked out of every fight with her head held high. Fey was a fighter—and a damn good one at that—and sword in hand, nothing could stop her. Her hands trembled, so she balled them into fists only to feel the tension slide from all the blood.

A hundred panicked voices, screaming for safety all at once, a cacophony of thought and emotion that could not be muffled to an open mind. Amethyst flame. Blackiron teeth. Crushed hope, abandoning a blue sky above and red dirt beneath.

Fey pushed the memory back again and screamed into ship that could not hear.
