Session 6

It took an hour for Fey to jerry rig the gravplate back online, mostly because she stopped every ten minutes to check that Alisa was still breathing. Once she was sure the bleeding had stopped, Fey had unceremoniously fastened Alisa to a bed in a spare quarters using cargo straps, then handcuffed her good hand to the bedframe for good measure. The assassin was only breathing shallowly by the time the gravity was back on, but she was breathing. The same couldn't be said for the former captain of the Elara Five.

Aparna had been lain down in the only room that could have been hers. While Fey's room was cozy if utilitarian, Aparna's room was filled with trinkets and adorned with art. It was colorful, with a faint smell of cigarettes and incense that the scrubbers couldn't quite absorb. The woman's body was completely enveloped in a patchwork quilt, well-loved and well-repaired over many years.

Fey looked at the strange lump of fabric. She would begin to smell before they got back to Meridian. Fey knew she couldn't keep her around, but the memory was still fresh enough to be "her" rather than "it." While Fey hadn't known Aparna for long, but from what she gathered, she wasn't a bad woman. At least, in her retirement she wasn't. Who could say what she'd done as a spy—after all, she had been assassinated, not simply murdered.

Fey closed the door to Aparna's cabin and sighed, then leaned her head back against the cold metal bulkhead. She sighed. The only leads she had right now was Alisa, who had a 50/50 shot of either dying without saying a word or living and still not saying anything useful. Fey's other lead was to talk to |Argus. A dark thought crossed Fey's mind.

What if the Spider had hired Alisa?

The man was jittery and seemed anxious, but what if that was all act? Aparna had implied that Argus had a soft spot for his informants, but the best lies and manipulations were built around granules of truth. Fey banged the back of her head against the bulkhead again.

If that were true, it was too late. The only charted leyline in and out of Penumbra went straight to Crux. As soon as the Elara Five dropped from the drift, Argus would know. Maybe if she was quick enough, she could gun it to the next sector, but then what?

"Never was good in the flight sim anyway," Fey muttered to herself. She pushed herself from the bulkhead and made her way to the bridge.

No, the only way out was to take the lion by the jaw. Alisa on her own was a source of information only if she lived. Fey had tried to tap unconscious minds before, and they yielded as much data as being blindfolded in a senseless void. She'd have to go back to Meridian and get a dockside doctor to fix the woman. And maybe, best case scenario, Argus would be as surprised as Fey was and would be able to help.

Worst case scenario, Fey had her sword.

Fey sat at Aparna's console and looked over the controls. The young woman had rudimentary training in flying craft from her time at the academy, but that was all just schooling. Thankfully, most craft had standardized after crossing into the Forge, and while it took several moments to familiarize herself with this layout, Fey was cautiously optimistic that she could at least avoid crashing.

Fey tapped at a monitor, flipping through the rest of the ship's status. While the gravplate had gone offline during the fight, apparently the torch drives were still burning. At least the gravplate and the torch drive's inertial dampeners were on separate loops. Fey turned to another monitor displaying the system map. She fiddled with its knobs, panning and zooming the map to take stock of their position, then after finding the leyline's jagged gash, took the ship's yoke and adjusted her course.
